I sell houses, so you would expect me to say buy. I will not say that to everybody, because it is not right for everybody. A soldier here for eighteen months with a deployment in the middle has a different answer than a family that plans to retire in Lawton.

First, who gets to choose

Single soldiers E-5 and below normally live in the barracks. If that is you, the rest of this page waits until you promote, marry, or get an exception.

Everybody else has all three options. The Army asks you to stop at the Housing Services Office before you sign a lease or a contract off post. It is in the Welcome Center, Building 4700, on the third floor. They keep rental listings and the off-limits list, and they help with landlord problems.

Living on post

Family housing on Fort Sill is run by Corvias. The neighborhoods are Buffalo Soldier Acres, Southern Plains, and Old Cavalry Post.

  • What it costs: your BAH. All of it goes to Corvias as rent, and that covers the house and most utilities.
  • What you skip: no deposit and no credit check.
  • Schools: on-post kids are in Lawton Public Schools. They attend Freedom Elementary, which is on post, then Central Middle School and Lawton High.
  • The commute: there is not one, and no gate line in the morning.
  • The catch: you do not pick from the whole market. What is open depends on your rank and family size. Call Corvias early at 580-581-2100 and ask about availability and any wait.
  • Pets: privatized Army housing restricts certain dog breeds. Ask Corvias about the pet rules before you count on it.

On post is the simplest choice. For a short tour or a first duty station, simple is worth a lot.

Renting off post

A single-family house in Lawton rents for somewhere around $1,200 to $1,300 a month. Utilities are on top of that. This is mostly a single-family market. There are apartments, but most families end up in a house.

  • If your BAH is above the rent and utilities, you keep the difference. On post, you would not.
  • If your BAH is below it, you are paying out of pocket to live off post.
  • You choose the house, the side of town, and the school district.
  • Your lease has a military exit. Federal law lets you end a lease with PCS or deployment orders. Give written notice with a copy of the orders.

Renting first is a reasonable plan if you have never seen Lawton. Six months in a rental teaches you which side of town you want.

  • What you get: part of every payment goes to principal instead of to a landlord, and the principal and interest do not go up.
  • What you take on: the roof, the water heater, and the yard are yours now. So is selling it or renting it when you leave.
  • What it takes up front: earnest money, inspections, and an appraisal. Sellers often cover closing costs.

How to decide

Three questions settle it for most people.

  1. How long will you be here? Buying and selling both cost money. Three years is tight for buying and then selling. Three years and you keep it as a rental is a different conversation.
  2. What happens to the house when you leave? Lawton has steady rental demand because of the post. Read keeping the house when you PCS.
  3. How much do you want to manage? Some people want a house that is theirs. Some want to hand back a key and be done. Both are fine.
