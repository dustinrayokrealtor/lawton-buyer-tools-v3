First, what this money is for. If you are using a VA loan there is no down payment, so "down payment assistance" really means help with closing costs and the prepaid insurance and taxes due at the table. On FHA it also covers the 3.5% down.

These programs come through lenders, not through me. What I can do is tell you they exist, tell you what to ask, and help you compare them to the other way of covering your costs, which is the seller.

REI Oklahoma Home100

REI Oklahoma runs its Home100 program statewide. On a VA, FHA, or USDA loan it gives you a gift toward your costs.

  • How much: 3% to 5% of the loan amount, depending on the option you pick.
  • Repayment: none on the gift options. It is not a lien.
  • First-time buyers only: no.
  • Credit score: 640.
  • Income limit: for Comanche County in 2026 it was $130,650 for a household of one or two and $152,425 for three or more.
  • The house: has to be your primary residence.

The trade is the interest rate. Generally, the more assistance you take, the higher the rate on the loan. That is how the gift gets paid for.

OHFA

The Oklahoma Housing Finance Agency is the state's program. It gives 3.5% of the loan toward your down payment and closing costs.

  • How it is structured: a second lien on the house. No interest and no monthly payment.
  • When it comes due: when you sell, refinance, pay off the first loan, or stop living in the house as your primary residence.
  • Two versions: Gold has the lower rate and is for first-time buyers, with tighter income and price limits. Dream is open to repeat buyers with income up to $150,000.
  • Credit score: 640 on government loans.
  • Special rates: a small discount for teachers and school staff, first responders, and state employees. There is not one for military.

Older articles call OHFA's assistance a grant. It is not one now. Plan on paying it back out of the sale.

Why the difference matters at Fort Sill

Read the OHFA list again and look at the last trigger: when you stop living in the house.

A lot of Fort Sill buyers plan to keep the house as a rental when they PCS. With OHFA's assistance on the house, moving out and renting it makes the second lien come due. On a $180,000 loan that is $6,300 you would owe at a bad time.

REI's gift does not have that string. If there is any chance you will keep the house when you leave, that should weigh heavily.

The third option: the seller

Before you take a higher rate for assistance, look at what a seller can do.

On a VA loan the seller can pay your normal closing costs, and can add up to 4% of the value in concessions on top for prepaid taxes and insurance. On FHA it is up to 6% of the price. Sellers here agree to that often, especially on houses that have been on the market a while.

When the seller covers your costs, you keep the plain loan and the plain rate. On a thirty-year loan even a small difference in rate adds up. So the order I would work in:

  1. Ask the seller to pay closing costs in the offer.
  2. If that leaves a gap, or the seller will not move, look at REI's gift.
  3. Look at OHFA if you are a first-time buyer who plans to stay put and the Gold rate is good that day.

Have your lender price the loan each way and put the payments side by side.

What to ask a lender

  • Are you approved for both REI Home100 and OHFA? Not every lender is.
  • What is my rate with no assistance, with REI at each level, and with OHFA?
  • What is the payment for each?
  • Does my household income fit under the limit, counting everyone who will live there?
  • If I rent the house out in three years, what happens?

Program terms change, and the limits get updated every year. Your lender has the current sheet. Everything here is as of fall 2026.
