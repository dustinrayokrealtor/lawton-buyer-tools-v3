The rate is the Freddie Mac weekly average as of {{ratedate}}, and these numbers move with it. If you have a VA disability rating of 10% or higher the funding fee is waived and the payment drops to about {{pay price=250000 ff=0}}. For FHA or conventional, run it in the payment toolkit.

At this price the funding fee is real money: $5,375 on a first use with nothing down. If you have a disability rating of 10% or more you do not pay it. If you do not, putting 5% down cuts the fee to 1.5%.

Who it fits

For nearly every pay grade this takes more than BAH, so this is a base pay or two-income house. That is normal. Lenders qualify you on everything you make, and BAH, BAS and disability pay count for more because they are not taxed.

[[bah:short]]

Every pay grade has its own page from the BAH table. If your allowance is under the payment, that does not rule the house out. Base pay and a spouse's income count too, and the VA loan calculator runs the whole household.

The number that decides a file at this price is usually residual income, which is what is left each month after the payment and your other debts. For a family of four in Oklahoma the VA wants at least $1,003 left over. Ask your lender to run it before you shop.

What changes above the median

  • Ask about days on market. There are usually fewer buyers above the median than below it, which can mean more room to negotiate. I can pull how long houses at this price have been taking to sell.
  • Newer houses. You start seeing newer construction, including new builds in Elgin and Geronimo. A newer roof helps your insurance quote.
  • Builders deal differently. A builder may hold firm on price and still pay closing costs or buy down your rate. The 2-1 buydown page explains how to weigh that.
  • Think about resale. If your tour is three years, think about who buys this house from you, or whether it rents for enough to keep.

Renting it out later

Be straight with yourself on this one. A single-family house here rents for around $1,200 to $1,300 a month, and a bigger, newer house rents for more than that, but usually not enough to cover a payment at this price. If keeping it as a rental is the plan, ask me for rent comps on the specific house first. It may still be the right house. You should just know the gap before you sign. More on that in keeping the house when you PCS.

The cash it takes

  • Down payment: none on a VA loan.
  • Earnest money: a deposit with your offer. It comes back as a credit at closing.
  • Inspections and appraisal: a home inspection, a termite inspection, and $750 for the VA appraisal.
  • Closing costs: roughly 2% to 5% of the price, which is $5,000 to $12,500 here. A big piece of that is the first year of insurance. Sellers often pay some or all of it. The closing costs page goes line by line.

Is it worth stretching to this

That is your call, and I will not push you up. Every extra $100 a month is roughly {{per100}} more house, so the difference between $200,000 and $250,000 is real money every month. If the house at $250,000 is the one you would stay in or keep, it can be worth it. If you are stretching because the lender said you could, it usually is not.
