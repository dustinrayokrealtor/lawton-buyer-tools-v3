Who it fits

For most pay grades this price takes more than BAH alone. It fits senior NCOs, officers, and households with a second income.

How the market is moving

  • Median time on the market is about {{dom}} days. Half go faster than that.
  • There are about {{supply}} months of supply. That has been tighter than the country as a whole.
  • Sellers have been getting about 98% of what they ask.

What that means for you: a good house does not sit, and there is usually not much room to cut the price. There is often room to have the seller pay closing costs or buy down your rate, which does more for your payment anyway. The buydown calculator shows the difference.

Houses that have been on the market a while are where the room to negotiate is. Finding those is my job.

What to look at

At the median you start to have choices. Newer against bigger. In town against a few miles out. A school district you want against a shorter drive. Before you look, decide which of those matters most to you, because you will not get all of them in one house.

  • The roof's age. It is one of the biggest things in your insurance quote.
  • The school district on the specific address. It follows the address, not the mailing city. See school districts.
  • The drive. Do it at the time you would really be going to work. See gates and commute.
  • The towns outside Lawton. Elgin, Cache and Geronimo are all worth a look at this number.

The cash it takes

  • Down payment: none on a VA loan.
  • Earnest money: a deposit with your offer. It comes back as a credit at closing.
  • Inspections and appraisal: a home inspection, a termite inspection, and $750 for the VA appraisal.
  • Closing costs: roughly 2% to 5% of the price, which is $4,000 to $10,000 here. A big piece of that is the first year of insurance. Sellers often pay some or all of it. The closing costs page goes line by line.

One more number

Every extra $100 a month you can put toward the payment is roughly {{per100}} more house. If $200,000 is close but not quite the house you want, that is the math on stretching. Just make sure the payment you end up with is one you are comfortable with. The most a lender will approve is usually more than that.
