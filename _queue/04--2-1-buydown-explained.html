The 2-1 buydown, explained

A 2-1 buydown drops your payment for the first two years using money the seller puts up at closing. It is a real tool, and it does not always beat the other ways to spend that money. Here is how it works and how to tell.

When a seller agrees to help with your costs, you get to choose how that money works for you. It can come off the price. It can buy your rate down for good. Or it can fund a 2-1 buydown, which is the one that takes the most explaining.

How it works

Your loan has a real interest rate. It is on the note and it never changes. With a 2-1 buydown you make payments as if the rate were 2% lower in the first year and 1% lower in the second. In year three you pay the real rate, and you keep paying it for the rest of the loan.

The difference does not vanish. Somebody puts the money in an escrow account at closing, and each month that account makes up the gap between what you pay and what the lender is owed. Usually that somebody is the seller. A builder or a lender can do it too.

What it costs

The cost is the total of your savings over the two years, paid up front. Here is a $200,000 loan with a 7% rate:

YearYou pay as ifPrincipal and interestSavings per month
One5%$1,074$257
Two6%$1,199$132
Three on7%$1,331$0

Twelve months at $257 plus twelve at $132 is about $4,660. That is what the seller deposits, and it works out to around 2.3% of the loan. The percentage stays close to that at any rate you are likely to see.

What it does not do

  • It does not help you qualify. The lender approves you on the full payment, the one you will have in year three. If the full payment does not fit, a buydown does not fix that.
  • It does not lower your rate. The note rate is the note rate.
  • It does not last. Your payment goes up at the first anniversary and again at the second. Put those dates on a calendar.

How it counts against the limits

Every loan type caps what a seller can give you.

  • VA: concessions up to 4% of the value. A seller-funded buydown counts toward that 4%. Your normal closing costs do not.
  • FHA: up to 6% of the price, all in.
  • Conventional: 3% with less than 10% down, more with a bigger down payment.

So a buydown at about 2.3% fits under every cap, with room left for closing costs on VA and FHA.

Buydown, points, or price

The same dollars can do three different jobs.

OptionWhat you getBest when
2-1 buydownThe biggest cut in the payment, for two years onlyMoney is tight right now and you expect it to ease, or you expect to refinance if rates fall
Permanent pointsA smaller cut in the rate, for the life of the loanYou will keep this loan a long time
Price cutA slightly smaller loan and a slightly lower paymentRarely the best use of the money for your payment. It does lower what you owe.

To put numbers on the last one: $4,660 off the price of a $200,000 house lowers the principal and interest by about $30 a month. The same $4,660 in a buydown lowers it by $257 a month in year one. That is why I usually suggest asking for the concession instead of the price cut, and then deciding how to use it.

For military buyers there is one more angle. If you might PCS in two or three years, you could be gone about the time the buydown runs out, which means you got the cheap years and never paid much of the full rate. It also means permanent points may never pay for themselves. For a short tour, the temporary buydown often makes more sense than points.

If you sell or refinance early

The money in the buydown account is yours in the sense that it cannot go back to the seller. If you pay off the loan before it is used up, by selling or refinancing, the unused part is generally applied to what you owe. The buydown agreement says exactly how. Read it.

When it fits and when it does not

It fits when the full payment is comfortable and the first two years are when cash is tightest. New furniture, a spouse still looking for work, a promotion coming.

It does not fit when the full payment is a stretch. If you need the buydown to make the house affordable, the house is too expensive. That is the test.

Common questions

How much does a 2-1 buydown cost?

About 2.2% to 2.3% of the loan amount. On a $200,000 loan with a 7% rate it comes to roughly $4,660. The cost is simply the total of the payment savings over the two years, paid in advance.

Do I qualify at the lower rate with a 2-1 buydown?

No. The lender qualifies you at the full note rate, which is the payment you will have from year three on. The buydown makes the first two years cheaper. It does not help you qualify for more house.

What happens to the buydown money if I sell or refinance early?

The unused part is generally applied toward what you owe when the loan is paid off. The buydown agreement spells it out, so read that page before you sign.
