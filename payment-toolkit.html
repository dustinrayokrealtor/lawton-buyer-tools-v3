Before you use this at the table

The rate drop per point is a placeholder until the lender on the file gives you a real cost sheet. Lenders price points differently every week, and a temporary 2-1 buydown works nothing like a permanent one. Concession caps also run by program: VA 4% of value, FHA 6%, conventional 3% under 10% down and 6% at 10% or more. Confirm all of it with the lender before you count on it.

Where the local numbers come from
  • Property tax, 1.11%. Lawton's median effective rate from Comanche County records. A specific parcel can run higher or lower, and homestead exemption isn't in here. Pull the real number off the assessor site before you settle on a payment.
  • Insurance, $19.28 per $1,000 of coverage. Built from Lawton's city average of $4,903 a year at $300,000 of dwelling coverage, scaled down to the coverage a median Lawton home actually carries. Works out to about $3,860 a year, or $321 a month, on a $199,500 house. That's lower than the Oklahoma state average, which blends in the OKC metro and assumes a lot more coverage than a house here needs.
  • Dwelling coverage at 100% of price. In a low-cost market, rebuild cost lands at or above market value, because lumber and labor don't get cheaper just because the house does. Adjust it if the lot carries real value.
  • VA funding fee. 2.15% first use with less than 5% down, 3.3% on subsequent use, financed into the loan. Zero if you draw service-connected disability compensation or holds a Purple Heart on the closing date.
  • FHA. 1.75% upfront premium financed in, plus 0.55% a year with less than 5% down, for the life of the loan. At 10% or more down it drops to 0.50% and falls off after 11 years.
  • Conventional PMI. 0.60% a year is a mid-credit placeholder. It falls off at 78% loan to value. Actual pricing swings hard on credit score, so get the real quote.
What this tool is not

This is an estimate for shopping, not a loan estimate, a pre-approval, or a quote. Taxes, insurance, and mortgage insurance all get set by somebody other than me. Your lender runs the real numbers, and those are the ones that go in an offer.
